Introduction
Introduction
Imagine an autonomous vehicle approaching an intersection — its camera blinded by glare, its LIDAR
picking up an obstacle, and its V2V signal delayed. Each onboard AI agent makes a decision. Each says it's
85% confident. But confident about what, exactly — and confident together?
This disconnect between individual agent confidence and collective trustworthiness is exactly where
modern multi-agent AI systems fall short.
Consider ARIA — an Autonomous Road Intersection Agent deployed at
a busy urban crossing.
She receives:
A camera feed (partially obscured by rain),
A LIDAR scan (reliable but low-resolution at range),
A V2V broadcast from a nearby vehicle (with 40ms network delay),
A map-based prediction model (trained on dry-road data only).
Each sub-agent returns a point estimate: "Brake now — 91% confident."
But none of them communicate why they're uncertain, what kind of
uncertainty they carry, or whether their confidence is even calibrated.
By the time a rare, unseen scenario unfolds, the system proceeds with false certainty — and there is no mechanism to
escalate to a human.
This gap between prediction and trustworthy decision-making creates catastrophic risk in high-stakes AI deployments.

Problem Statement
Problem Statement-
To design a calibrated, multi-agent uncertainty quantification framework that decomposes agent-level
confidence into aleatoric and epistemic components, propagates uncertainty across agents using graph-
based belief networks, applies conformal prediction to provide mathematically guaranteed confidence
intervals, and enables transparent, attributed decision-making with structured human escalation — all
within a real-time (<100ms) constraint.

Objectives
1. Framework Development: Design and implement a complete 5-module Bayesian multi-agent system for end-to-end
uncertainty quantification and decision- making.
2. Uncertainty Modeling: Decompose uncertainty into aleatoric (irreducible data noise) and epistemic (reducible model
uncertainty) at the individual agent level.
3. Confidence Calibration: Provide mathematically guaranteed and reliable confidence intervals using conformal prediction
techniques.
4. Efficient Communication: Enable intelligent and uncertainty-aware communication between agents using Graph Neural
Networks (GNNs).
5. Uncertainty Attribution: Identify and quantify the contribution of each agent to overall system uncertainty using Shapley
value-based methods .
6. Multi-Domain Validation: Evaluate system performance across real-world domains such as autonomous driving (CARLA),
medical diagnosis (CheXpert), and drone swarm search & rescue .
7. Regulatory Compliance: Ensure the system meets modern AI safety and transparency standards such as ISO 26262, EU AI Act
2.0, and FDA guidelines.

Methodology - Proposed Approach
End-to-End Uncertainty-Aware Decision Pipeline
1. Mult i-Agent Input Acquisition
Collect sensor data , agent predictions , and environmental context required for decision-making.
2. Uncertainty D ecomposition
Separate uncertainty into aleatoric (data noise) and epistemic (model uncertainty) using ensemble and MC dropout
techniques .
3. Bayesian Belief Fusion
Integrate predictions from multiple agents through probabilistic inference and reliability-based weighting .
4. Conformal Calibration
Calibrate model outputs to generate statistically reliable confidence intervals with a 95% coverage guarantee.
5. Uncertainty-Aware Communication
Enable trust-based information exchange between agents using Graph Neural Networks (GNN) .
6. Decision Making & Attribution
Apply risk thresholds to determine actions , compute confidence scores , and identify the most uncertain contributing agent
7. Output Generation & Feedback
Produce final decisions with uncertainty breakdown and continuously improve system performance via feedback loops .

=== PAGE 23 ===
Summary
Cognix is designed to solve a problem that existing AI systems largely ignore the inability to
explain why they are uncertain and what should be done about it. The system operates across
five interconnected modules, where each agent first decomposes its sensor observations into
two distinct uncertainty components, fuses its beliefs with neighbouring agents through
probabilistic message passing, receives a mathematically guaranteed calibration ensuring
stated confidence levels are accurate, communicates only the most informative uncertainty
signals to other agents through a learned graph-based protocol, and finally receives a
complete decision output that not only recommends an action but also identifies which
specific agent is the weakest link in the collective reasoning chain. Applied to autonomous
driving, medical diagnosis, and multi-robot coordination, Cognix moves AI decision making
away from blind confidence toward a model of structured and explainable self-awareness,
where every prediction carries a justified level of trust rather than an arbitrary score.
